# Fact-Check: "mRNA vaccine DNA integrated into chromosome 19" (Hulscher / Ask Dr. Drew clip)

## What the clip claims

In a clip from *Ask Dr. Drew*, Nicolas Hulscher (McCullough Foundation) states:

> "We identified a chimeric segment of Pfizer/Moderna genetic code — part of the
> spike-encoding sequence from the DNA plasmid — was literally integrated in
> chromosome 19. It is a perfect 20/20 base-pair match of the Pfizer reference
> sequence... about a one-in-a-trillion chance of being an anomaly. So this is
> literally the first [case] of genomic integration of mRNA vaccine genetic
> material. We found a stage-four bladder cancer patient — a 31-year-old woman...
> she's got this turbo cancer. We profiled her circulating tumor DNA, ran
> proteomics... multi-omic analysis."

## Verdict

**The paper is real, but the headline claim is not reliable evidence.**

The study exists: *"Genomic Integration and Molecular Dysregulation in Aggressive
Stage IV Bladder Cancer Following COVID-19 mRNA Vaccination,"* Catanzaro, Hulscher
& McCullough, *International Journal of Innovative Research in Medical Science*,
Oct 2025. It is a **single-patient case report** — the weakest tier of medical
evidence. The "vaccine DNA integrated into chromosome 19" conclusion rests on
findings that mainstream geneticists consider far more consistent with a lab
artifact than with a real biological event.

## Claim-by-claim

### 1. "Integrated into chromosome 19" — based on a single chimeric read
The evidence is one "host–vector chimeric read" in plasma cell-free DNA. A lone
chimeric read is the textbook signature of a **sequencing/library-prep artifact**
(PCR chimeras, ligation artifacts, template-switching), which occur routinely in
next-generation sequencing. Demonstrating genuine integration requires orthogonal
validation — junction-spanning PCR confirmed by Sanger sequencing, multiple
independent reads, and confirmation in tumor *tissue*, not just plasma. None of
that is shown.

### 2. "Perfect 20 base-pair match" — short, and not unique to the vaccine
The spike sequence is **derived from the SARS-CoV-2 virus itself**, so a short
match isn't proof of a *vaccine* origin (prior or concurrent infection contains
spike too). Twenty base pairs is also short enough that matches arise readily
across a 3-billion-bp genome when scanning millions of reads. Internal oddity: a
"Pfizer reference" match in a patient who received **Moderna** — the two products
use different codon-optimized spike sequences.

### 3. "One in a trillion chance of being an anomaly" — misuse of statistics
The figure looks like (1/4)^20 ≈ 1-in-a-trillion, i.e. the odds of a *random*
20-letter DNA string. But the sequence isn't random (it's biological/conserved),
and that number is not a valid probability that the read represents real
integration. It conflates "odds this exact 20-mer occurs by chance at one spot"
with "odds this is genuine integration."

### 4. "First documented genomic integration of mRNA vaccine material"
mRNA can't integrate into DNA on its own — it would need reverse transcription.
The claim leans on **residual DNA-plasmid contamination**, a separate and partly
legitimate issue (raised by Kevin McKernan; regulators acknowledge trace residual
DNA while maintaining it is within safety limits). The only prior lab hint of any
DNA effect is Aldén et al. (2022), an *in vitro* liver-cell-line study — heavily
caveated and never shown in a living person.

### 5. "Turbo cancer"
Not a recognized scientific or clinical term. Cancer registries and large
epidemiological datasets (FDA, EMA, the Global Vaccine Data Network) show **no
post-rollout surge** in aggressive cancers. Stage IV bladder cancer in a
31-year-old is genuinely rare — but rare events happen, and one anecdote cannot
establish causation. The "molecular dysregulation" genes cited (KRAS, PIK3CA,
ATM, etc.) are commonly mutated in many cancers regardless of vaccination.

## Important context

- **Conflicts of interest.** All three authors are tied to the McCullough
  Foundation, an anti-mRNA-vaccine advocacy group. The proprietary assays used
  ("PBIMA"/"REViSS") are products of co-author Catanzaro's own company — not
  standard, independently validated methods.
- **The journal.** IJIRMS appears on Beall's List of likely **predatory
  journals**. The promotion leans heavily on "peer-reviewed," but that label
  carries little weight here.
- **Causation.** The authors themselves concede a single case can't prove the
  vaccine caused the cancer. Temporal sequence (vaccine → later cancer) is not
  causation.
- **Plasmid background.** A plasmid is a small circular DNA loop used as a lab
  "photocopier" template. The Pfizer manufacturing process transcribes mRNA from
  a DNA plasmid template, which is then digested/filtered out. Cleanup is not
  100% perfect, so trace plasmid-DNA fragments can remain — real and acknowledged
  by regulators. The unproven leap is that such fragments integrated into the
  patient's chromosomes.
- **Unrelated chyron.** One screenshot's chyron ("FDA warns of autism risk from
  Tylenol") is a separate, contested claim; a large 2024 sibling-controlled study
  found no causal acetaminophen–autism link.

## Bottom line

A real but very weak (single-case, predatory-journal, conflicted-authors) report
is being promoted as definitive proof that mRNA vaccines integrate into human DNA
and cause cancer. The central "integration" finding is a single chimeric read with
no orthogonal validation — most plausibly a sequencing artifact — and the
"1-in-a-trillion" and "perfect match" framings are statistically misleading. It
does not establish that mRNA vaccines alter DNA or cause cancer.
